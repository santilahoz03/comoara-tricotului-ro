Welcome

How to Use This Book

30 patterns organized by category — pick any one and start today.

Every pattern in this book was designed for real homes, tested by real crafters, and written so you can follow along stitch by stitch — even if you're picking up a hook for the first time.

What You'll Find in Each Pattern

  • Difficulty rating — Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced — so you can pick projects that match your skill level.
  • Materials list — Exact yarn weight, hook size, and any extras you'll need.
  • Finished measurements — So you know what you're making before you start.
  • Step-by-step instructions — Written in standard US crochet terminology with stitch counts at the end of every row.
  • Pro tips — Practical advice to get professional results.

Before You Begin

  1. Browse the Table of Contents and pick a category that excites you.
  2. Start with a Beginner pattern if you're new, or jump to Intermediate/Advanced if you're confident.
  3. Read the full pattern once before you begin — it takes 5 minutes and saves hours of frogging.
  4. Make a gauge swatch. Seriously. It takes 10 minutes and ensures your finished piece is the right size.
  5. Check the bonus guides for yarn selection, color matching, and decoration tips.
Gauge MattersAlways make a 10×10 cm swatch in the stitch pattern before starting a project. If your swatch is too large, go down a hook size. Too small? Go up. This one step is the difference between a perfect fit and a do-over.

1. Classic Seed Stitch Cushion Cover

A timeless textured cover with a buttoned envelope back — the perfect first home decor project.

Materials

  • Worsted weight cotton yarn (cream/natural), 250 g / ~460 m
  • 5.0 mm (H/8) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle
  • 3 buttons, 20 mm diameter
  • 45 × 45 cm cushion insert

Gauge & Finished Size

16 sts × 16 rows = 10 cm in seed stitch

Finished: 45 × 45 cm (fits a standard 45 cm insert snugly)

Front Panel

  1. Foundation: Ch 73.
  2. Row 1 (RS): Sc in 2nd ch from hook, *dc in next ch, sc in next ch; rep from * across. (72 sts)
  3. Row 2: Ch 1, turn. *Dc in sc, sc in dc; rep from * across. (72 sts)
  4. Rows 3–72: Rep Row 2. The seed stitch pattern naturally alternates — each sc sits on top of a dc and vice versa.
  5. Fasten off. Piece measures approximately 45 × 45 cm.

Back Panel (Envelope Style, Two Pieces)

  1. Upper Back: Ch 73. Work Rows 1–42 in seed stitch as above. (42 rows × 72 sts)
  2. Lower Back: Ch 73. Work Rows 1–38 in seed stitch. (38 rows × 72 sts)
  3. Button band (on Lower Back): Row 39: Ch 1, turn. Sc across. Row 40: Ch 1, turn. Sc 10, *ch 3, sk 3, sc 18; rep from * once more, ch 3, sk 3, sc 10. (3 buttonholes evenly spaced) Row 41: Ch 1, turn. Sc across, working 3 sc into each ch-3 sp.

Assembly

  1. Block all three pieces to 45 cm wide. The Upper Back is ~26 cm tall; the Lower Back is ~24 cm tall (including button band).
  2. Lay the Front panel RS up. Place the Upper Back on top, RS down, aligning the top edges. Place the Lower Back RS down, aligning the bottom edges. The two back pieces overlap by ~5 cm in the center.
  3. Pin all layers together. Using a tapestry needle, whip stitch around all four edges through all layers.
  4. Turn right side out through the envelope opening. Sew buttons on the Upper Back to align with the buttonholes.
  5. Insert the cushion form.
Pro TipUse a hook one size smaller for the button band — it creates a firmer edge that holds buttons better and won't stretch out over time.
Intermediate40 × 40 cm~8 hours

2. Bobble Texture Throw Pillow

Rows of raised bobbles create a satisfying 3D texture that photographs beautifully on any sofa.

Materials

  • Worsted weight acrylic-cotton blend (dusty rose), 220 g / ~400 m
  • 5.5 mm (I/9) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle
  • 40 × 40 cm cushion insert

Gauge & Finished Size

14 sts × 12 rows = 10 cm in bobble pattern

Finished: 40 × 40 cm

Bobble: Yo, insert hook, pull up a loop, yo, pull through 2 loops — repeat 4 more times in same st (6 loops on hook), yo, pull through all 6 loops.

Front Panel

  1. Foundation: Ch 57.
  2. Row 1 (RS): Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (56 sts)
  3. Row 2 (WS): Ch 1, turn. Sc across. (56 sts)
  4. Row 3 (RS — bobble row): Ch 1, turn. Sc 3, *bobble in next st, sc 3; rep from * to last st, sc 1. (13 bobbles, 56 sts)
  5. Rows 4–5: Ch 1, turn. Sc across. (56 sts)
  6. Row 6 (RS — offset bobble row): Ch 1, turn. Sc 5, *bobble in next st, sc 3; rep from * to last 3 sts, sc 3. (12 bobbles, 56 sts)
  7. Rows 7–8: Ch 1, turn. Sc across.
  8. Rows 9–48: Rep Rows 3–8 six more times, then rep Rows 3–6 once. Fasten off.

Back Panel

  1. Ch 57. Work in sc for 48 rows. (56 sts per row) Fasten off.

Assembly

  1. Block both panels to 40 × 40 cm.
  2. Hold panels together with WS facing. Sc through both layers around 3 sides.
  3. Insert cushion form. Sc the 4th side closed.
  4. Weave in all ends.
Pro TipPush each bobble to the RS as you work it — they naturally want to pop to the back. A gentle poke with your finger after completing each bobble keeps the texture on the right side.
Intermediate50 cm diameter~10 hours

3. Round Floor Cushion

A generous floor cushion worked in the round — perfect for reading nooks and kids' rooms.

Materials

  • Bulky weight cotton yarn (sage green), 400 g / ~480 m
  • 7.0 mm crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle, stitch marker
  • Polyester fiberfill, ~500 g
  • Round cardboard disc, 48 cm diameter (optional, for structure)

Gauge & Finished Size

11 sts × 6 rnds = 10 cm in dc spiral

Finished: 50 cm diameter × 10 cm thick

Top Circle

  1. Magic ring.
  2. Rnd 1: 8 dc into ring. Sl st to join. (8 sts)
  3. Rnd 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), dc in same st, 2 dc in each st around. Sl st to join. (16 sts)
  4. Rnd 3: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 1, *2 dc in next st, dc 1; rep from * around. Sl st to join. (24 sts)
  5. Rnd 4: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 2, *2 dc in next st, dc 2; rep from * around. Sl st to join. (32 sts)
  6. Rnds 5–14: Continue increasing pattern (inc 8 sts per rnd, with one more dc between increases each rnd). After Rnd 14: 112 sts. Circle measures ~50 cm diameter.
  7. Rnd 15 (texture): Ch 3, *FPdc around next dc, dc in next dc; rep from * around. Sl st to join. (112 sts)

Bottom Circle

  1. Work identical to Top Circle, Rnds 1–14 (plain dc, no texture round). (112 sts)

Side Band

  1. Starting from the top circle, Rnds 16–21: Ch 3, dc in each st around (no increases). (112 sts per rnd, 6 rnds = ~10 cm)

Assembly

  1. Hold the bottom circle against the open edge of the side band, WS together.
  2. Sc through both layers around ¾ of the circumference.
  3. Insert cardboard disc (optional) and stuff firmly with fiberfill.
  4. Sc the remaining ¼ closed. Fasten off. Weave in ends.
Pro TipStuff the cushion extra firmly — it will compress 20–30% once someone sits on it. The cardboard disc inside the bottom keeps the cushion from sagging into a blob shape.
Advanced50 × 30 cm~12 hours

4. Cable Twist Lumbar Pillow

Front post cables create a knit-look texture that elevates any chair or bed — the show-off piece of your collection.

Materials

  • Worsted weight merino-acrylic blend (oatmeal), 200 g / ~370 m
  • 5.0 mm (H/8) crochet hook
  • Cable needle or spare hook
  • Tapestry needle
  • 50 × 30 cm lumbar cushion insert

Gauge & Finished Size

16 sts × 10 rows = 10 cm in cable pattern

Finished: 50 × 30 cm

Cable Cross (over 4 sts): Sk 2 sts, FPdc around each of the next 2 sts, FPdc around each of the 2 skipped sts (working in front of the sts just made).

Front Panel

  1. Foundation: Ch 81.
  2. Row 1 (RS): Dc in 4th ch from hook and in each ch across. (78 sts + ch-3 counts as dc = 79 dc total; but we'll count 78 working sts)
  3. Row 2 (WS): Ch 3, turn. Dc across. (78 sts)
  4. Row 3 (cable set-up, RS): Ch 3, turn. Dc 5, *FPdc around each of next 4 sts, dc 6; rep from * 6 more times, dc to end. (78 sts, 7 cable panels)
  5. Row 4: Ch 3, turn. Dc across (working BPdc around FPdc of previous row to maintain post definition).
  6. Row 5 (cable cross, RS): Ch 3, turn. Dc 5, *cable cross over next 4 sts, dc 6; rep from * 6 more times, dc to end.
  7. Rows 6–8: Rep Rows 2–4 (post stitches without crossing).
  8. Rows 9–30: Rep Rows 5–8 five more times, then rep Row 5 once. Fasten off. Piece measures ~50 × 30 cm.

Back Panel

  1. Ch 81. Work in dc for 30 rows. (78 sts per row) Fasten off.

Assembly

  1. Wet block the front panel — the cables pop more after blocking.
  2. Hold panels WS together. Sc through both layers around 3 sides, insert form, sc the 4th side closed.
Pro TipCables look complex but they're just rearranged front post stitches. Practice the cable cross on a swatch of 20 stitches before starting the full panel — after 3 repeats it becomes muscle memory.
Beginner45 × 45 cm~8 hours

5. Granny Square Patchwork Cushion

Nine classic granny squares joined into a cushion — a colorful way to use up leftover yarn and learn the most iconic stitch pattern.

Materials

  • DK weight cotton yarn in 4 colors (cream 100 g, sage 60 g, dusty rose 60 g, mustard 40 g)
  • 4.0 mm (G/6) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle
  • 45 × 45 cm cushion insert

Gauge & Finished Size

Each square: 15 × 15 cm after blocking

9 squares (3 × 3) = 45 × 45 cm

Granny Square (Make 9)

  1. With Color A (mustard), magic ring.
  2. Rnd 1: Ch 3, 2 dc in ring, ch 2, *3 dc in ring, ch 2; rep from * twice. Sl st to top of ch-3 to join. (4 groups of 3 dc, 4 ch-2 corners) Fasten off Color A.
  3. Rnd 2 (Color B — dusty rose): Join in any corner ch-2 sp. Ch 3, 2 dc in same sp, ch 2, 3 dc in same sp (corner made), *ch 1, [3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc] in next corner sp; rep from * around, ch 1. Sl st to join. Fasten off Color B.
  4. Rnd 3 (Color C — sage): Join in any corner ch-2 sp. Ch 3, 2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc in same sp, *ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, [3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc] in corner sp; rep from * around. Sl st to join. Fasten off Color C.
  5. Rnds 4–5 (Color D — cream): Continue the granny pattern, adding one more 3-dc group per side each round. After Rnd 5: 4 groups of 3 dc per side + 4 corner groups. Square measures ~15 × 15 cm.

Assembly

  1. Block all 9 squares to exactly 15 × 15 cm.
  2. Arrange in a 3 × 3 grid. Join using flat slip stitch or whip stitch through back loops in cream yarn.
  3. Work 2 rnds of sc around the entire joined front panel in cream to create a clean border.
  4. For the back panel: Ch 73, work in sc for 72 rows in cream. Block to 45 × 45 cm.
  5. Hold front and back WS together, sc around 3 sides, insert form, sc closed.
Pro TipWind each color into small balls before starting — it keeps the tension even and prevents the tangles that happen when you pull from 4 different skeins. Also: always join new colors in a corner space, never mid-side.

6. Chunky Basketweave Throw

Front and back post stitches create a woven-look texture that's reversible — both sides look great.

Materials

  • Bulky weight acrylic yarn (linen/natural), 1200 g / ~1440 m
  • 6.5 mm (K/10½) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

12 sts × 8 rows = 10 cm in basketweave pattern

Finished: 130 × 170 cm

Stitch count must be a multiple of 8 + 2.

  1. Foundation: Ch 158. (156 + 2 for turning)
  2. Row 1 (RS): Dc in 4th ch from hook and in each ch across. (156 dc)
  3. Row 2: Ch 3, turn. *FPdc around each of next 4 sts, BPdc around each of next 4 sts; rep from * across to last 4 sts, FPdc 4. (156 sts)
  4. Rows 3–5: Rep Row 2 (same post stitch placement).
  5. Row 6: Ch 3, turn. *BPdc around each of next 4 sts, FPdc around each of next 4 sts; rep from * across to last 4 sts, BPdc 4. (156 sts — the blocks are now offset)
  6. Rows 7–9: Rep Row 6.
  7. Rows 10–137: Rep Rows 2–9 sixteen more times. Fasten off.
  8. Border: Join yarn in any corner. Rnd 1: Sc evenly around all 4 sides, with 3 sc in each corner. Sl st to join. Rnd 2: Rep Rnd 1. Fasten off.
Pro TipThis pattern uses a lot of yarn — buy all your skeins from the same dye lot. If you can't, alternate skeins every few rows to blend any slight color differences.
Beginner100 × 130 cm~20 hours

7. Ripple Stitch Lap Blanket

Classic chevron waves in three colors — a meditative project that grows quickly and looks stunning folded on a chair.

Materials

  • Worsted weight acrylic yarn: Color A (cream) 300 g, Color B (sage) 200 g, Color C (dusty rose) 200 g
  • 5.5 mm (I/9) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

14 sts × 8 rows = 10 cm in ripple pattern

Finished: 100 × 130 cm

Foundation chain must be a multiple of 14 + 3.

  1. Foundation (Color A): Ch 143. (14 × 10 + 3)
  2. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook, dc in next 4 ch, *dc3tog over next 3 ch, dc in next 5 ch, 3 dc in next ch, dc in next 5 ch; rep from * across, ending dc2tog in last 2 ch. (140 sts)
  3. Row 2: Ch 3, turn. Dc in first st (increase), dc 5, *dc3tog, dc 5, 3 dc in next st, dc 5; rep from * across, ending dc2tog. (140 sts)
  4. Rows 3–4: Rep Row 2 in Color A.
  5. Rows 5–8: Change to Color B. Rep Row 2 for 4 rows.
  6. Rows 9–12: Change to Color C. Rep Row 2 for 4 rows.
  7. Rows 13–104: Continue the 4-row color stripe sequence (A, B, C) for a total of ~104 rows. Fasten off.
Pro TipCount your stitches at the end of every row — the ripple pattern relies on a constant stitch count. If you're gaining or losing stitches, you've missed an increase (3-dc group) or decrease (dc3tog).
Beginner120 × 150 cm~25 hours

8. Modern Moss Stitch Throw

Also called the linen stitch — creates a dense, drapey fabric with a woven look that works in any room.

Materials

  • Worsted weight cotton-acrylic blend (oatmeal), 900 g / ~1650 m
  • 5.0 mm (H/8) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

16 sts × 14 rows = 10 cm in moss stitch

Finished: 120 × 150 cm

Foundation chain: even number + 1.

  1. Foundation: Ch 193. (192 + 1)
  2. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, *ch 1, sk 1, sc in next ch; rep from * across. (96 sc + 96 ch-1 sps = 192 sts)
  3. Row 2: Ch 1, turn. Sc in first sc, *ch 1, sk ch-1 sp, sc in next sc; rep from * across. Wait — instead: sc in first sc, *ch 1, sc in next ch-1 sp (the space, not the ch); rep from * across, sc in last sc.
  4. Correction — Row 2: Ch 1, turn. Sc in first st, ch 1, sk next st, *sc in ch-1 sp, ch 1, sk sc; rep from * across, sc in last st. (192 sts)
  5. Rows 3–210: Rep Row 2. The moss stitch creates a checkerboard of sc and ch-1 spaces. Fasten off.
  6. Border: Sc evenly around all 4 sides, 3 sc in each corner. Sl st to join. Fasten off.
Pro TipThe moss stitch uses more yarn than you'd expect because of all the chain-1 spaces. Buy 10% more yarn than you think you'll need. The good news: the fabric barely stretches, so it keeps its shape without blocking.
Intermediate120 × 150 cm~30 hours

9. Corner-to-Corner Ombré Blanket

The trendy C2C technique worked in a gradient from dark to light — a modern statement piece.

Materials

  • Worsted weight acrylic yarn in 5 shades of sage/green (darkest to lightest): 200 g each
  • 5.5 mm (I/9) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

1 C2C block = ~2.5 cm square

Finished: ~120 × 150 cm (48 × 60 blocks)

C2C block: Ch 6, dc in 4th ch from hook and in next 2 ch. (1 block = 3 dc on a ch-3 base)

  1. Starting corner (Color 1 — darkest): Ch 6. Dc in 4th ch from hook and in next 2 ch. (1 block)
  2. Increase rows: Each row adds 1 block. Turn, ch 6, dc in 4th ch from hook and next 2 ch (new block), *sl st into ch-3 sp of block below, ch 3, 3 dc in same sp; rep for each block in previous row. Continue increasing for 48 rows (48 blocks on the longest diagonal).
  3. Color changes: Change color every 10 rows — Color 1 (rows 1–10), Color 2 (11–20), Color 3 (21–30), Color 4 (31–40), Color 5 (41–48 and decreasing rows).
  4. Straight rows: Work 12 rows without increasing or decreasing (maintaining 48 blocks per row) to create the rectangular shape.
  5. Decrease rows: Each row loses 1 block. At the start of the row, sl st across the first block instead of working it. Continue decreasing for 48 rows until 1 block remains. Fasten off.
  6. Border: 2 rnds of sc around all 4 sides in the lightest color, 3 sc in each corner. Fasten off.
Pro TipC2C grows faster than you think — the diagonal start makes the first rows feel slow, but by row 20 each row takes 20+ minutes. Plan your color transitions before you start so you don't run out of one shade early.
Intermediate130 × 180 cm~35 hours

10. Fringed Boho Sofa Blanket

Wide stripes in earthy tones with long, lush fringe on both short ends — the statement piece for a boho living room.

Materials

  • Worsted weight cotton yarn: Color A (natural/cream) 500 g, Color B (terracotta) 300 g, Color C (mustard) 200 g, Color D (sage) 200 g
  • 6.0 mm (J/10) crochet hook
  • Large cardboard or book (~20 cm wide, for cutting fringe)
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

13 sts × 7 rows = 10 cm in hdc

Finished: 130 × 180 cm (before fringe)

Fringe adds ~15 cm per end.

  1. Foundation (Color A): Ch 170.
  2. Row 1: Hdc in 3rd ch from hook and in each ch across. (168 hdc)
  3. Row 2: Ch 2, turn. Hdc in each st across. (168 hdc)
  4. Stripe sequence: 20 rows Color A, 12 rows Color B, 8 rows Color C, 12 rows Color D, 20 rows Color A, 12 rows Color B, 8 rows Color C, 12 rows Color D, 20 rows Color A. Total: 124 rows. Fasten off.
  5. Side border: Sc evenly along both long sides (1 sc per row end). Fasten off.
  6. Fringe: Cut strands 40 cm long. Using 4 strands per bundle: fold in half, insert hook through foundation chain edge, pull fold through, pull ends through loop, tighten. Space bundles every 2 stitches across both short ends. (~84 bundles per end)
  7. Trim fringe evenly to ~18 cm.
Pro TipWrap yarn around a hardcover book to cut fringe quickly and evenly. Count how many wraps give you the right number of strands, then cut along one edge. For a boho look, separate the plies of each strand with a pet brush or wide-tooth comb after attaching.

11. Lace Diamond Runner

Open lacework diamonds framed by a solid border — elegant on a dark wood dining table.

Materials

  • DK weight cotton yarn (white or ecru), 200 g / ~480 m
  • 4.0 mm (G/6) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle, blocking pins

Gauge & Finished Size

1 diamond repeat = ~8 cm wide × 6 cm tall

Finished: 35 × 120 cm after blocking

  1. Foundation: Ch 121. (Multiple of 16 + 9)
  2. Row 1 (RS): Dc in 4th ch from hook, dc 2, *ch 2, sk 2, dc 1, ch 2, sk 2, dc 1, ch 5, sk 5, dc 1, ch 2, sk 2, dc 1; rep from * across, ending dc 4. (7 diamond repeats)
  3. Row 2: Ch 3, turn. Dc 3, *ch 2, dc in next dc, ch 2, dc in next dc, ch 2, 5 dc in ch-5 sp, ch 2, dc in next dc; rep from * across, ending dc 4.
  4. Row 3: Ch 3, turn. Dc 3, *ch 2, sk ch-2 sp, dc in next dc, ch 2, dc in first dc of 5-dc group, dc 3, dc in last dc of group, ch 2, dc in next dc; rep from * across, ending dc 4.
  5. Row 4: Ch 3, turn. Dc 3, *ch 2, dc in next dc, ch 5 (sk the 5-dc group to form the top of the diamond), dc in next dc, ch 2, dc in next dc; rep from * across, ending dc 4.
  6. Rows 5–80: Rep Rows 1–4 nineteen more times. (20 full diamond pattern repeats vertically) Fasten off.
  7. Border: Join in corner. Rnd 1: Sc evenly around, 3 sc in corners. Rnd 2: *Sc 3, ch 3, sl st in first ch (picot), sc 3; rep from * around. Fasten off.
  8. Blocking: Wet block and pin firmly — lace patterns open up dramatically after blocking.
Pro TipLace runners must be blocked to look their best. Pin them out on a foam mat, spray with water, and let them dry completely. The diamonds will open up and the edges will straighten — before blocking, lace looks like a crumpled mess. This is normal.
Beginner30 × 150 cm~10 hours

12. Farmhouse Stripe Runner

Simple stripes in two colors with a clean border — the quickest runner to make and the most versatile.

Materials

  • Worsted weight cotton: Color A (cream) 150 g, Color B (grey-blue) 100 g
  • 5.0 mm (H/8) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

15 sts × 14 rows = 10 cm in sc

Finished: 30 × 150 cm

  1. Foundation (Color A): Ch 46.
  2. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (45 sc)
  3. Row 2: Ch 1, turn. Sc across. (45 sc)
  4. Stripe pattern: 6 rows Color A, 2 rows Color B. Repeat this 8-row sequence 26 times (208 rows total, ~150 cm). Fasten off.
  5. Border: With Color B, sc evenly around all 4 sides, 3 sc in each corner. Sl st to join. Fasten off.
Pro TipDon't cut the yarn at each color change — carry the unused color loosely up the side. It saves yarn, time, and ends to weave in. The border hides the carried strands.
Advanced35 × 130 cm~20 hours

13. Filet Crochet Floral Runner

A classic filet mesh with filled-in blocks forming a floral motif — heirloom-quality work.

Materials

  • Size 10 crochet thread (white or ecru), 300 m
  • 1.75 mm (US 5 steel) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle, blocking pins, starch spray

Gauge & Finished Size

10 filet blocks = ~8 cm; 10 rows = ~8 cm

Finished: 35 × 130 cm after blocking and starching

Open mesh: dc, ch 2, sk 2, dc. Filled block: 4 dc (no chain space).

  1. Foundation: Ch 133. (44 blocks × 3 + 1)
  2. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook and in each ch across. (132 dc = 44 blocks of 3 dc, all filled)
  3. Rows 2–8 (border): Filled blocks at edges (3 blocks each side), open mesh in the center (38 open blocks).
  4. Rows 9–155 (floral chart): Follow the grid — each open mesh = ch 2, sk 2, dc; each filled block = 3 dc. The floral pattern repeats 3 large roses with leaves, connected by vines. The border of 3 filled blocks continues on each side throughout.
  5. Rows 156–162: Mirror the opening border (Rows 2–8). Fasten off.
  6. Finishing: Wet block aggressively. Apply light starch spray. Pin to exact measurements and let dry completely.
Patience RequiredFilet crochet with thread is slow — expect 3–4 rows per hour. The result is worth it, but don't start this as your first runner. Practice the technique with worsted weight yarn on a small sample first.
Intermediate30 × 120 cm~12 hours

14. Minimalist Geometric Runner

Clean lines and negative space — a modern runner using only chains and double crochets in a grid pattern.

Materials

Gauge & Finished Size

18 sts × 9 rows = 10 cm in pattern

Finished: 30 × 120 cm

15. Scallop Edge Runner

16. Round Mandala Placemat

Concentric rings of texture radiating from the center — each placemat is a mini mandala.

Materials (per placemat)

  • DK weight cotton yarn (natural), 80 g / ~190 m

Gauge & Finished Size

Rnds 1–4 = 8 cm diameter

Finished: 35 cm diameter

  1. Magic ring.
  2. Rnd 1: Ch 3, 11 dc in ring. Sl st to join. (12 dc)

  4. Rnd 3: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 1, *2 dc in next, dc 1; rep from * around. Sl st. (36 dc)
  5. Rnd 4: Ch 1, sc in each st. Sl st. (36 sc — creates a texture ring)
  6. Rnd 5: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 2, *2 dc, dc 2; rep from * around. Sl st. (48 dc)
  7. Rnd 6: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 3, *2 dc, dc 3; rep from * around. Sl st. (60 dc)
  8. Rnd 7: Ch 1, sc in each st. Sl st. (60 sc — second texture ring)
  9. Rnd 8: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 4, *2 dc, dc 4; rep from * around. Sl st. (72 dc)
  10. Rnd 9: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 5, *2 dc, dc 5; rep from * around. Sl st. (84 dc)

  12. Rnd 11: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 6, *2 dc, dc 6; rep from * around. Sl st. (96 dc)
  13. Rnd 12: Ch 3, dc in same st, dc 7, *2 dc, dc 7; rep from * around. Sl st. (108 dc)
  14. Rnd 13: Ch 1, *sc 1, ch 2, sk 1; rep from * around. Sl st. (54 sc + 54 ch-2 sps — lace ring)

Pro TipIf your placemat is ruffling (wavy edges), you're increasing too fast — try going down a hook size. If it's cupping (bowl-shaped), you need more increases — go up a hook size or add an extra increase round.

24. Square Mosaic Coasters (Make 4)

Two-color mosaic crochet creates a bold geometric pattern — a conversation starter at any dinner party.

Materials (for 4 coasters)

  • DK weight cotton: Color A (cream) 30 g, Color B (charcoal) 30 g
  • 3.5 mm (E/4) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

20 sts × 20 rows = 10 cm in mosaic sc

Finished: 10 × 10 cm

Mosaic rule: Only one color per row. Skip sts in the current color to let the previous row's color show through.

  1. Foundation (Color A): Ch 21.
  2. Row 1 (A): Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (20 sc)
  3. Row 2 (B): Ch 1, turn. Sc 2, *sk 1 (letting Color A show), sc 3; rep from * across, ending sc 2. (20 sts)
  4. Row 3 (A): Ch 1, turn. Sc 3, *sk 1 (letting Color B show), sc 3; rep from * across, ending sc 1. (20 sts)
  5. Row 4 (B): Ch 1, turn. Sc across (all sc, no skips — solid row). (20 sts)
  6. Rows 5–20: Continue alternating colors every row, following a simple diamond/cross chart: sk the sts where you want the other color to show through. The diamond forms naturally over 8 rows.
  7. Border (Color B): Sc around all 4 sides, 3 sc in corners. Sl st. Fasten off.
Pro TipIn mosaic crochet, the skipped stitch is simply not worked — you don't chain over it. The stitch from the row below naturally fills the gap. Keep your tension firm so the "peeking" stitches sit flat.
Intermediate12 × 8 cm~40 min each

25. Leaf Coasters (Make 4)

Shaped like a leaf with a stem — a nature-inspired coaster that works beautifully in sets of 4.

Materials (for 4 coasters)

  • DK weight cotton (olive green), 40 g / ~95 m
  • 3.5 mm (E/4) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle

Gauge & Finished Size

Finished: ~12 cm long × 8 cm wide

  2. Rnd 1 (work around both sides of chain): Sc in 2nd ch from hook, hdc in next, dc in next 3, tr in next 3, dc in next 3, hdc in next, sc in next, 3 sc in last ch (tip of leaf). Pro TipThe graduated stitches (sc → hdc → dc → tr → dc → hdc → sc) create the natural leaf shape. For variety, make each coaster in a slightly different shade of green — sage, olive, forest, and lime.
